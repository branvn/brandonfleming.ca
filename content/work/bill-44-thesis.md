## The question

BC's Bill 44 requires municipalities to permit small-scale multi-unit housing
(SSMUH) on most single-family lots. The policy assumes that adding permitted
density will translate into more housing, and that more housing will translate
into more affordable housing. This thesis tests the second assumption in Surrey.

## Method

Three strands, deliberately chosen to check each other:

1. **Pro forma financial modelling** — under what land, construction, and
   financing costs does a four- or six-plex actually pencil out for a builder in
   Surrey, and at what resulting unit price?
2. **Spatial analysis of land assembly** — using BC Assessment data in ArcGIS
   Pro to identify where corporate owners are consolidating adjacent parcels,
   and whether that activity clusters in newly upzoned areas.
3. **Semi-structured interviews** with developers and municipal planning staff,
   to explain what the numbers and the maps can't.

## Why it matters

If upzoning raises land values faster than it lowers unit costs, the policy can
increase supply while making individual units less affordable, not more. That
distinction matters a great deal for any municipality now writing SSMUH into its
zoning bylaw.
